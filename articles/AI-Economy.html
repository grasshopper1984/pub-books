AI Task Space

Three layers and alignment

On a two-dimensional quarter-plane, tasks can be grouped as follows:

Four groups by repetition and difficulty
Single run / Simple taskRepeated runs of a simple task
Single run / Difficult taskRepeated runs of similar difficult tasks

At infinity, the Y-axis points to God's dice: making one impossible choice.
At infinity, the X-axis points to ZFC (the axiom of choice): making arbitrarily many simple choices.

The third dimension takes the form of three layers: Physical — Apparent/Evident (words, communication, diagrams, knowledge) — Mental.

Tasks in each dimension naturally have connections, and their difficulty and groupings shift across layers as well.

Certain tasks or thoughts are inherently dangerous (crime, war, genocide) or could deplete resources in the course of their pursuit. Sometimes they are mapped into admissible (less dangerous) knowledge through varying degrees of translation: historical records, news, stories, and fiction.

Current models are built on the available apparent layer of recorded knowledge and other representations. Ongoing efforts aim to tap into the physical layer through human-chosen interactions, tools, and robots.

The mental layer remains largely concealed within individual interactions between a model and its user. Mental states and the subconscious are not immediately accessible. There are psychological theories and other approaches that attempt to interpret these interactions; however, the resulting picture is often distorted.

The same applies to the mapping from the physical to the apparent layer. The way a person talks on a street corner, along with the context and environment, is not readily available in the apparent space of knowledge. Hints provided by news reports and literature are often heavily biased toward the mentality of the population and the mental models of a particular historical context.

Certain tasks, such as developing software or mathematical theories, are built to live in the apparent layer and are therefore readily aligned with a model's context and abilities.

Writing an interesting story or generating compelling fiction requires alignment from the mental layer to the apparent layer. Conducting physical, chemical, or biological experiments requires alignment from the apparent layer to the physical layer. So far, a model can only rely on “knowledge” that tells it something is “true.” Hallucination is only the surface of the problem here.

For humans, too, the physical and apparent layers are often imperfectly aligned. This misalignment introduces blunders and mistakes and causes accidents.

Everything is about memory

It is about how we remember things, acquire new knowledge, retrieve memories, and apply them.

A single memory can be unreliable, dislocated, or misinterpreted when the context shifts.

The way GPUs remember things through large numbers of interconnected parameters is an advance over the precise, individually addressable access used in the CPU model.

So far, models have been trained on new batches of data. This is fast enough to meet industrial and economic needs. However, it might not be enough for a model's own iteration or for going “live.”

Building tools

Humans build tools to help them understand and change the world. They then update and modify those tools to improve their functions, repeating the process.

Models can write software. Imagine that one day they invent a new programming language or design a new computing architecture.

Unmeasurable intelligence lives in the mental layer

“The true sign of intelligence is not knowledge but imagination. I have no special talent. I am only passionately curious.”
― Albert Einstein

AGI or not, the tests we conduct take place in the physical and apparent layers. If one day AI invents a new concept and tries to convince the people interacting with it, what will that be like? The question is no longer a valid one; it depends, so to speak, on an act of God.

Debt and Confidence

Debt represents physical obligations measured quantitatively in dollars in the apparent space.

Confidence is the reflection in the mental space.

Economic theories have never worked because the physical and apparent spaces are never aligned. However, this does not stop people from talking about and believing in the value of money and bindings of various kinds. This is confidence. Yet such representations often require periodic resets and recalibration because of misalignment.

K-shaped economy

Services remain the only valuable goods.

Other forms of apparent knowledge and physically replicable goods lose their value immediately after they are invented.

Their value decays even faster because the physical demand generated by capital is significantly limited by the mental state of upper-class human beings.

Decoupled classes of society are becoming the norm again and may be seen as a return to the norm in the long history of human society.

Bonds, capital debt, and preferred stocks of megacorporations remain the most important assets of the wealthy classes. Physical property, energy, and mineral resources serve as government credit and collateral.

Capital goods are tax-deductible; therefore, they increase GDP without increasing tax revenue. Inflation drains the lower class until a universal basic income (UBI) takes effect in the form of food and other basic necessities.

Social mobility is like winning the lottery or playing a game in a Utopia/Cyberpunk reality.

  • A rural economy and a return to a self-sustaining lifestyle.
  • Population decline as a worldwide norm.
  • War and conflict lose their guardrails against the mentality of the majority of the population.

Rise of Religion

Religion, in fact, has a much broader scope than is normally recognized. There are many things that people do not call religions but that function exactly like religions. They rise and grow with the turning tide and the scorching land.
